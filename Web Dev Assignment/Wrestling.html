Definition of Wrestling

Wrestling is a combat sport involving grappling techniques such as clinch fighting, throws and takedowns, joint locks, pins, and other grappling holds.
It can be practiced in various styles such as freestyle, Greco-Roman, and traditional forms found in different cultures.

Five Basic Rules of Wrestling

  1. Scoring:
    Points are awarded for takedowns, escapes, reversals, and near falls. The wrestler with the most points at the end of the match wins.
  2. Pin (Fall):
    A match can be won instantly by pinning the opponent's shoulders to the mat for a specified period.
  3. Illegal Moves:
    Certain actions such as striking, eye-gouging, and biting are prohibited.
  4. Period Duration:
    Matches are divided into periods, usually two or three, each lasting two to three minutes depending on the style.
  5. Weight Classes:
    Competitions are divided into weight classes to ensure fair matches between wrestlers of similar sizes.

Five Prestigious Wrestling Awards Globally

  1. Olympic Medals:
    Winning an Olympic gold, silver, or bronze medal is considered one of the highest achievements in wrestling.
  2. World Wrestling Championships:
    Medals awarded at this annual event are highly prestigious.
  3. Continental Championships:
    Titles such as the Asian, European, and Pan American Championships are significant accolades.
  4. Golden Grand Prix:
    A series of tournaments organized by United World Wrestling that attract top wrestlers from around the world.
  5. Hall of Fame Inductions:
    Being inducted into the National Wrestling Hall of Fame or the UWW Hall of Fame is a lifetime achievement.
