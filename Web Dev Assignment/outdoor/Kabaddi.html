Definition of Kabaddi

Kabaddi is a contact team sport that originated in ancient India. It involves two teams of seven players each, where one player from the attacking team (the raider) enters the opponent's half of the court to tag as many defenders as possible and return to their own half without being tackled. The game requires agility, strength, and strategic play.

Five Basic Rules in Kabaddi

  1. Raid:
    The raider must chant "Kabaddi, Kabaddi" continuously while attempting to tag opponents and return to their half without being caught. Failing to do so results in losing the raid.
  2. Tagging and Scoring:
    The raider earns points by tagging defenders and successfully returning to their side. Defenders earn points by tackling and stopping the raider.
  3. Out and Revival:
    Tagged defenders are out of the game until a raider from their team scores, at which point they can re-enter the game.
  4. Bonus Line:
    In some formats, crossing the bonus line with at least one foot in the opponent's court while chanting "Kabaddi" scores an extra point.
  5. Time Limit:
    Each raid has a time limit, usually 30 seconds. If the raider exceeds this time or is tackled before returning, the raid is unsuccessful.

Five Major Global Competitions in Kabaddi

  1. Kabaddi World Cup:
    An international tournament organized by the International Kabaddi Federation (IKF), featuring teams from around the world.
  2. Asian Games:
    Kabaddi is a regular event in the Asian Games, with both men's and women's teams competing.
  3. Pro Kabaddi League (PKL):
    A professional Kabaddi league in India that has gained international popularity and attracts top players from various countries.
  4. Kabaddi Masters:
    An invitational tournament featuring top Kabaddi-playing nations.
  5. South Asian Games:
    A multi-sport event where Kabaddi is a prominent sport, with teams from South Asian countries competing.

Five Notable Indian Achievements in Kabaddi

  1. Multiple World Cup Titles:
    India has won multiple Kabaddi World Cup titles in both men's and women's categories.
  2. Asian Games Dominance:
    The Indian men's and women's teams have consistently won gold medals at the Asian Games since Kabaddi was introduced.
  3. Pro Kabaddi League:
    The success of the Pro Kabaddi League has popularized the sport and showcased India's top Kabaddi talent on a global stage.
  4. Star Players:
    Legendary players like Anup Kumar, Rahul Chaudhari, and Rakesh Kumar have achieved significant milestones and contributed to India's dominance in Kabaddi.
  5. International Recognition:
    Indian Kabaddi players have gained international recognition and are often invited to participate in global Kabaddi leagues and tournaments.
