Definition of Chess

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on an 8x8 checkered board. Each player begins with 16 pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two knights, two bishops, and eight pawns. The objective of the game is to checkmate the opponent's king.

Five Basic Rules of Chess

  1. Movement:
    Each type of piece moves in a specific way. For example, pawns move forward but capture diagonally, knights move in an "L" shape, and bishops move diagonally.
  2. Check and Checkmate:
    The game aims to put the opponent's king in check, meaning it is under direct threat of capture. Checkmate occurs when the king cannot escape capture.
  3. Castling:
    A special move involving the king and one of the rooks, allowing both to move simultaneously under specific conditions.
  4. En Passant:
    A special pawn capture that can occur if a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting position and lands beside an opponent's pawn.
  5. Promotion:
    When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board, it can be promoted to any other piece (except a king), usually a queen.

Five Prestigious Chess Awards Globally

  1. World Chess Championship:
    The most prestigious title in chess, awarded to the world champion.
  2. Chess Olympiad:
    A biennial team event where national teams compete for medals.
  3. Candidates Tournament:
    The tournament that determines the challenger for the World Chess Championship.
  4. Grandmaster Title:
    The highest title awarded by FIDE (the International Chess Federation) to the best chess players.
  5. FIDE World Cup:
    A knockout tournament that offers qualification spots to the Candidates Tournament.

Five of India's Achievements in Chess

  1. Viswanathan Anand:
    Five-time World Chess Champion and the first Indian to achieve the Grandmaster title.
  2. Koneru Humpy:
    The second woman to achieve the Grandmaster title and a former Women's World Rapid Chess Champion.
  3. Pentala Harikrishna:
    Became a Grandmaster at a young age and has been a consistent performer in international tournaments.
  4. Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu:
    One of the youngest Grandmasters in the world, achieving the title at the age of 12.
  5. Dronavalli Harika:
    A top female Grandmaster who has won several medals at the Women's World Chess Championship and other international events.
