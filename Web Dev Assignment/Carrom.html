Definition of Carrom

Carrom is a popular tabletop game originating from South Asia, played on a square board with pockets at each corner. Players use a striker to flick small discs (carrom men) into the pockets.

Five Basic Rules of Carrom

  1. Striking:
    The striker must be flicked using one finger, and it must strike the carrom men to pocket them.
  2. Objective:
    Players aim to pocket all their assigned carrom men (either black or white) and the queen before the opponent.
  3. Queen:
    The queen can be pocketed at any time, but it must be followed by pocketing one of the player's own carrom men to cover the queen.
  4. Fouls: Fouls include pocketing the striker, pocketing the opponent's carrom men, or failing to cover the queen.
  5. Scoring:
    Points are awarded based on the number of carrom men pocketed, with the queen carrying bonus points.

Five Prestigious Carrom Awards Globally

  1. World Carrom Championship:
    The most prestigious tournament in the world of carrom.
  2. Asian Carrom Championship:
    A major competition featuring top players from Asia.
  3. European Carrom Championship:
    Highlights the best talent from Europe.
  4. Carrom World Cup:
    An international event showcasing top players globally.
  5. National Championships:
    Various countries hold national-level tournaments to crown their champions.

Five of India's Achievements in Carrom

  1. Maria Irudayam:
    A legendary Indian carrom player, two-time World Carrom Champion, and multiple-time national champion.
  2. A. Maria Irudayam:
    Dominated the carrom scene in the 1990s with numerous international and national titles.
  3. S. Ilavazhagi:
    An accomplished female player with several international titles, including the World Carrom Championship.
  4. Rashmi Kumari:
    Another leading female player, known for her consistent performances and multiple national and international titles.
  5. Indian Team Success:
    India has won numerous team championships, showcasing the country's dominance in the sport.
